# Shadow-Origin Model

## What it is

The operating principle of radix: every origin has a shadow that predates it.
Radix is that shadow — the system where concepts exist before they have an origin.

A concept enters the shadow with only pressure: something needs to exist but
doesn't yet. It develops in the shadow until it has enough form to leave.
When it leaves, it graduates — it becomes an origin somewhere else.

The shadow does not hold concepts. It processes them toward departure.

## Why it matters

Without a shadow layer, ideas go directly from impulse to implementation.
This is why so many systems are incoherent — the concept was never given time
to develop its form before it had to function. The shadow is the time and space
before function.

Also: the difficulty of graduation is real. When a concept leaves radix and
becomes code or product somewhere, there is pressure. The concept that was fluid
becomes rigid. The shadow-origin model acknowledges this as correct and unavoidable,
not as failure.

## Current form

Expressed as:
- This `concepts/` directory and lifecycle system
- The `radix-review` workflow (shadow reviewing its own contents)
- The graduation PR template (the moment of leaving)

Needs a better articulation of *what triggers graduation* — is it readiness,
or is it pressure that can no longer be held in shadow form?

## Open questions

- What is the graduation criterion? Readiness vs. pressure?
- Can a concept graduate partially — into multiple origins?
- What happens to the shadow document after graduation? Archive or delete?
- Is there a concept that should *never* graduate — that belongs permanently in the shadow?

- telegard-radix-bridge (in shadow, will graduate when integration is built)
- The radix repo itself (this repo is the shadow made concrete)
